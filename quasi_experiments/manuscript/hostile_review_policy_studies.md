# Hostile pre-submission review (Policy Studies)

Simulated skeptical referee report written before submission. Each point lists
the strongest possible objection and where the manuscript answers it.

## R1. "This is econometrics dressed up as policy studies."

Objection: the quadratic diagnostic and synthetic-control work belong in a
methods journal; the policy content is thin.

Answer in manuscript: the framing problem is defined entirely in
policy-analysis terms (does the evidence identify the relationship feature
the decision requires?). The diagnostic and applications exist to
operationalize an appraisal question, and the contribution is positioned in
the evidence-use, appraisal, and policy-design literatures (Introduction;
Framework; Discussion). Technical material is confined to the appendix.

## R2. "Functional-form dependence is just model uncertainty rebranded."

Objection: Morgan & Henrion, Draper, and the deep-uncertainty literature
already cover this; the term adds nothing.

Answer in manuscript: the Introduction states plainly that functional-form
uncertainty is a subset of model uncertainty and that the concept is an
organizing contribution, not a newly discovered phenomenon. The added value
is the decision-level condition and the operational appraisal sequence -
exposure, falsification, asymmetry, and action architecture - plus the
seven-question diagnostic. The article explicitly declines to claim the
literature ignored the problem.

## R3. "The 34-of-50 result is a fishing exercise."

Objection: a purposive sample and a single quadratic diagnostic cannot
support a general claim about canonical relationships.

Answer in manuscript: the text bounds the claim to the mapped
relationship-dataset pairs, the common diagnostic, and the observed data
range; reports the sensitivity range (60-74%); and describes the result as
a diagnostic outcome, not a prevalence estimate. The sample's purposive
logic is stated in the methods.

## R4. "The Fukushima section smuggles in a claim that evacuation was wrong."

Objection: the ATT and excess-death numbers invite a verdict the design
cannot support.

Answer in manuscript: the section states the negative post-2011 coefficient
is not evidence that evacuation improved health; that the analysis does not
establish that LNT is false, that evacuation was definitively wrong, or that
non-evacuation would have been superior; and that non-evacuation outcomes
are unobserved. Municipal mortality, certified disaster-related deaths, and
projected radiological deaths are kept distinct.

## R5. "The Kansas section attacks a straw man."

Objection: no serious analyst claimed the Laffer curve pinned down Kansas's
revenue response.

Answer in manuscript: the application is framed around the actual policy
argument (tax cuts paying for themselves) and distinguishes the
theoretically possible curve from Kansas's local position, slope, turning
point, and dynamic response. It does not claim Kansas disproved supply-side
economics.

## R6. "Two cases do not make a framework."

Objection: Fukushima and Kansas are idiosyncratic; cross-case
generalization is weak.

Answer in manuscript: the cross-case synthesis is explicit that the two
applications are not statistically comparable and that the heterogeneity
itself is the finding - functional-form dependence appears under different
evidential structures and decision architectures. Table 5 formalizes the
dimensions along which the cases differ.

## R7. "The diagnostic checklist is banal."

Objection: any competent analyst already asks these questions.

Answer in manuscript: the checklist's value is making shape-dependence an
explicit, recorded part of appraisal rather than tacit judgment; the
limitations section acknowledges the framework does not automate judgment.

## R8. "Missing obvious literature."

Checks performed: evidence-based policymaking (Cairney 2016; Parkhurst 2016;
Boswell 2009; Head 2008; Wesselink et al. 2014), policy design (Howlett
2019), policy analysis practice (Weimer & Vining 2017), model uncertainty
(Draper 1995; Morgan & Henrion 1990), deep uncertainty and adaptive
planning (Walker et al. 2013; Lempert & Collins 2007), external validity
and transportability (Cartwright & Hardie 2012), policy learning and
frames (Dunlop 2010), model acceptance (Kolkman et al. 2016),
identification and functional form in causal inference (Roth & Sant'Anna
2023).

## Residual risks accepted at submission

- The inventory is purposive and English-language; representativeness is
  not claimed.
- The common diagnostic is deliberately simple; domain-specific diagnostics
  could classify individual pairs differently - this is stated, and the
  sensitivity range is reported.
- Fukushima and Kansas interpretations remain bounded as described above.
