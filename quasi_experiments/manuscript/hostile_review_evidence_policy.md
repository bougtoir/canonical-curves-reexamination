# Hostile five-perspective review (Evidence & Policy)

Simulated hostile referee report. Each reviewer is asked to find the
strongest available objection; responses record what the manuscript
already does and what was changed (if anything) in response.

## Reviewer A — evidence-use scholar (E&P core readership)

**Objection.** "This is a statistics paper wearing an evidence-policy
costume. The 50-curve mapping and the quasi-experiments are
methodological; the evidence-use literature is window dressing."

**Assessment.** The framing has been rebuilt: the research question is
about evidence specificity at the point of decision; the mapping is a
diagnostic of the gap, and both applications are organised around the
translation chain (evidence → assumed shape → decision → consequences),
each stage documented. The Discussion speaks separately to
policymakers, researchers, and knowledge brokers — the journal's three
audiences. The checklist is an appraisal tool, not an estimator.
Residual risk: a referee could still want more engagement with the
implementation/knowledge-broker literature. The focused review section
cites the journal's own publications (Ward, House and Hamer 2009,
Evidence & Policy) and Nutley et al. (2007); adding more would crowd
the word limit. Accepted risk, disclosed.

## Reviewer B — statistical referee

**Objection.** "The quadratic nested-F diagnostic with Cook's-distance
and LOO-CV is ad hoc; 68% 'not robust' is an artifact of arbitrary
criteria. The SCM/diff-in-diff cases are standard and prove nothing
about 'functional form'."

**Assessment.** The manuscript explicitly denies the mapping a
prevalence claim ("a diagnostic map, not a replication-failure rate")
and reports the full 60–74% sensitivity range rather than a point.
The two applications do not claim the estimates are novel — their role
is to locate the functional feature the decision required and test
whether the evidence identified it in range (e.g., only 6 observations
below 0.1 Gy; Laffer turning point outside observed rates). That is a
claim about identification scope, not about estimate magnitude, and is
correctly hedged. No change needed beyond existing hedges.

## Reviewer C — radiation-protection expert

**Objection.** "Attacking LNT-based evacuation while ignoring the
precautionary rationale and confounding by tsunami/disaster conditions.
Dangerous misreading of health physics."

**Assessment.** The manuscript states three calibrations verbatim: LNT
is not declared false; evacuation is not declared definitively wrong;
measured, certified, and projected deaths are kept as distinct
estimands (verified: the article never commingles the 2,350 certified
related deaths with the 936 measured 2011 excess or WHO/THJ
projections). Precaution under consequence asymmetry is handled
explicitly in the synthesis (who bears losses from underaction vs
overaction). Tsunami confounding is addressed by the >X% inundation
exclusion and T2 specification. Compliant.

## Reviewer D — public-finance economist

**Objection.** "Kansas is a straw man: nobody serious claimed Kansas
sat past the Laffer maximum; SCM placebo p=0.03 on IIT is cherry-picked
against GDP +1.5% (p=0.71)."

**Assessment.** The text reports all four outcomes with their
permutation p-values in the same paragraph — revenue decline beside
null GDP/employment effects — which is exactly the asymmetry the
argument needs and does not hide the nulls. The claim is not "Kansas
disproves Laffer" (stated verbatim) but that the defence rested on a
turning-point/slope proposition the evidence did not identify at
observed rates, under assumptions about tax-rate–revenue and growth
responses (documented via Laffer 2004 and Rothschild 2012). Compliant.

## Reviewer E — "this is replication crisis / theory debunking in disguise"

**Objection.** "68% failure of 'canonical' curves is a replication-
crisis headline; the paper debunks textbook theory while pretending to
be about policy."

**Assessment.** Blocked in the text: purposive design, no prevalence
estimate, no falsification claim ("does not falsify any underlying
theory"), and the mapping is framed as quantifying an
*evidence-specificity gap* — the question is whether evidence supports
the specificity a decision requires, not whether relationships are
real. Conclusion sentence required by the brief is present verbatim:
"Evidence should not be translated into a more specific policy claim
than the underlying data can support." Compliant.

## Verdict

No objection identified a defect requiring numeric, structural, or
claim changes. Residual exposures (limited knowledge-broker citation
depth; purposive mapping; two applications) are disclosed in
Limitations.
