# EVIDENCE_POLICY_FIT_DIAGNOSIS — JCPA → Evidence & Policy fit audit

Read as an Evidence & Policy editor. The JCPA manuscript is scientifically sound but framed for comparative policy analysis. Items below list current framing, why it is misaligned, and the E&P reframing applied in `text_evidence_policy.py`.

## Title
- Current: "When Policy Depends on a Curve: Comparing Functional-Form Dependence in Fukushima Evacuation and Kansas Tax Reform"
- Misaligned: foregrounds the two cases and "comparing"; reads as comparative policy scholarship, not evidence-use research.
- E&P framing: foreground evidence, specificity and translation. New title: "When Evidence Is Not Specific Enough for Policy: Functional-Form Dependence in Evidence-Based Decision-Making".

## Abstract
- Current: 94 words centred on the structured comparison and "transferable lessons for policy appraisal".
- Misaligned: comparison is the subject; the evidence-use problem is implicit.
- E&P framing: structured abstract (Introduction; Methods/Data; Analytic plan; Results; Conclusion/Implications) leading with the evidence-to-policy mismatch, the definition of functional-form dependence, the cross-domain mapping, the two applications, and the practical implication. ≤250 words.

## Introduction
- Current paragraphs 3-6: gap framed as absence of "a comparative account"; puzzle stated as "why does the same evidence problem create different decision risks in different policy settings"; cases introduced via maximum-contrast case-selection logic; contributions framed as conceptual/comparative/practical.
- Misaligned: the motivating problem is comparative-methodological, not evidence-use. An E&P reader sees a JCPA article retargeted.
- E&P framing: open with the translation problem — policy decisions require decision-relevant specificity (threshold, slope, turning point, curvature, extrapolation) that credible association-level evidence may not supply; define functional-form dependence as an evidence-use condition; locate it in evidence-informed policymaking, knowledge translation/brokerage, relevance and transportability literatures; the two cases are applications that show the problem in different domains; contributions = identify the mismatch, demonstrate it empirically across domains, derive an appraisal checklist.

## "Comparative research design" section
- Current: maximum-contrast selection, units of comparison, eight common comparative dimensions, structured-focused justification, N=2 defence.
- Misaligned: methods vocabulary written for JCPA reviewers; elevates design over the evidence problem.
- E&P framing: a short "Methods" section describing the study as cross-domain evidence-use analysis with two policy applications; retain what is needed to describe data and the diagnostic (mapping, municipal panel, state panel); reduce design terminology to a paragraph explaining purposive contrast across domains and its limits.

## Cross-domain mapping ("Background" subsection)
- Current: demoted to "background motivation", ~2 short paragraphs.
- Misaligned in the other direction: for E&P the mapping is primary empirical evidence that the specificity problem is not isolated — it deserves its own Findings subsection. Must still avoid prevalence/replication-failure language.
- E&P framing: first Findings subsection, "Mapping the evidence-specificity problem"; findings framed as an evidentiary specificity gap, not theory failure.

## Case sections ("Case 1"/"Case 2")
- Current headings name the cases; subheads are the eight comparative dimensions (assumed relationship, decision-relevant feature, decision sensitivity, consequence asymmetry, reversibility and learning).
- Misaligned: reads as comparative case-study anatomy.
- E&P framing: reframed as evidence-to-policy applications — "Fukushima: translating low-dose evidence into policy", "Kansas: translating fiscal-response evidence into policy"; the same moderating dimensions retained but described as moderators of the evidence-to-policy problem, not comparative dimensions; the translation chain (evidence → assumed shape → policy interpretation → decision → consequences outside the curve) made explicit.

## "Comparative findings" / propositions / lesson-drawing
- Current: common mechanism, divergence table, three propositions, lesson-drawing in Rose/Dolowitz-Marsh terms.
- Misaligned: comparative-policy-theory payload.
- E&P framing: single "Cross-case synthesis: when evidence specificity matters" subsection keeping Table 5 (recaptioned) and the moderators (decision sensitivity, consequence asymmetry, reversibility, transportability, observability/learning); propositions compressed into plain findings; lesson-drawing folded into the appraisal checklist in the Discussion.

## Discussion
- Current: "principal comparative finding", "what comparison adds", "implications for comparative policy analysis".
- Misaligned: addresses comparative methodologists.
- E&P framing: rebuilt around the E&P readership — principal finding (specificity required by policy can exceed specificity supported by evidence); why study quality is not enough; relevance and transportability; how translation inflates claims; implications for policymakers/practitioners (checklist), researchers, and knowledge brokers; limitations; conclusion ending on the evidence-to-policy principle.

## Cover letter
- Current: JCPA letter opening with the comparative question and maximum-contrast design.
- Misaligned: leads with cases and comparison.
- E&P framing: new letter leading with the specificity mismatch; explains relevance to researchers, policymakers, practitioners and knowledge brokers; cross-domain design as deliberate evidence of generality; actionable appraisal framework.

## Claim calibration (preserved)
- No claim that canonical theories are false, that 68% failed replication, that evacuation was disproven, that LNT is false, or that Kansas disproves the Laffer curve; policymaker reliance on curves is documented/interpretive distinction maintained (see POLICY_RATIONALE_SOURCE_AUDIT.md).
