# Evidence & Policy closest-literature audit

Purpose: identify the nearest published work in the journal's own
conversation, state what the manuscript adds beyond it, and confirm the
article's positioning is not a replication-crisis or comparative-methods
contribution in disguise.

## Closest literatures

### Evidence-informed policymaking and barriers to use

- Cairney (2016); Parkhurst (2016); Boswell (2009); Nutley, Walter and
  Davies (2007); Oliver et al. (2014); Ward, House and Hamer (2009).
- What they cover: whether evidence is credible, relevant, accessible,
  timely, contested, and used; institutional filters; knowledge
  brokering.
- What they do not cover: the specificity dimension — whether evidence
  identifies the *functional feature* (threshold, slope, turning point,
  extrapolated response) the decision requires. Barriers frameworks
  treat "the evidence" as a bundle; they do not separate identification
  of an association from identification of its shape in the
  policy-relevant range.
- Manuscript's addition: names this mismatch (functional-form
  dependence), quantifies an evidence-specificity gap across 50 named
  relationships, and operationalises it for appraisal via the
  seven-question checklist aimed at policymakers, researchers, and
  knowledge brokers.

### Model and functional-form uncertainty (statistics/risk)

- Draper (1995); Morgan and Henrion (1990); Lempert and Collins (2007).
- What they cover: model-form uncertainty as a component of scientific
  uncertainty; robust decision approaches.
- Difference: those literatures address how analysts should *manage*
  model uncertainty; this article studies how that uncertainty *enters*
  policy decisions when findings are translated — an evidence-use
  problem, not a methods prescription. Cited as grounding, not as the
  contribution.

### Transportability / evidence-based policy guides

- Cartwright and Hardie (2012).
- Closest single work: distinguishes "the policy works here" evidence
  from the support needed for "it will work there". This article's
  question is orthogonal and complementary: even *here*, the evidence
  may not identify the feature the decision needs. Cited in Background;
  the relationship is stated explicitly in the manuscript.

- Deliberately not engaged as literature. The mapping is purposive and

## Positioning statement verified in text

- Background paragraph on "a distinct level of mismatch … an
  evidence-specificity gap" distinguishes the article from both the
  barriers literature and the study-quality literature.
- Discussion subsection order follows the journal's audiences:
  principal finding → why study quality is not enough → relevance and
  transportability → translation → implications for policymakers,
  researchers, knowledge brokers → limitations → conclusion.

## Verdict

Sufficiently differentiated. No closer single published item was found
that (a) names the specificity mismatch between evidence and decision,
(b) quantifies it across domains, and (c) supplies an appraisal
checklist. The article belongs in Evidence & Policy's core
conversation, not in a methods journal.
